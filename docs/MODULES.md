# Module Outline

Working document. Structure agreed, content not 
written. Nothing here is final.

**Rule for every module:** define each acronym on 
first use. Explain requirements in original wording, 
never reproduce ISO clause text. If a paragraph 
could appear in a competitor's material with the 
name swapped, rewrite it.

---

## Module 1 - What just happened

**The reader's situation:** someone asked them about 
AI governance and they did not have an answer.

Covers: why this landed on their desk, what 
procurement and enterprise customers are actually 
asking for and why, what ISO 42001 is in one plain 
paragraph, what an AI management system is at a 
basic level, and what the standard is not.

**Exercise:** the existing readiness assessment tool 
on sydneyaiworks.com. Sets a baseline they return 
to in Module 4.

---

## Module 2 - The language

**The reader's situation:** every document they open 
is full of terms nobody has defined for them.

Covers: AIMS, control, control objective, risk 
register, impact assessment, statement of 
applicability, scope, internal audit, management 
review, nonconformance, continual improvement. Each 
explained in ordinary words with a concrete example, 
not a dictionary definition.

**Exercise:** interactive flashcard deck. Ten terms, 
ten plain English definitions, immediate feedback.

**Status: prototype built.** Committed to 
prototype/module-02-flashcards.html. Styled to 
match the readiness assessment tool.

---

## Module 3 - What the standard wants from you

**The reader's situation:** they have looked at the 
clause list and it means nothing.

Covers: the requirements regrouped by what you 
actually have to do, rather than by clause number. 
Roughly: know your context, decide who is 
accountable, work out your risks, write things down, 
operate the controls, check yourself, improve.

**Exercise:** scenario decisions against a fictional 
mid-size company. Reader makes calls, sees 
consequences, gets an explanation either way.

---

## Module 4 - Finding your gaps

**The reader's situation:** they need to know where 
their organisation actually stands, honestly.

Covers: how to run an internal assessment without a 
compliance function, who to talk to, what evidence 
looks like, how to record findings, and how to tell 
a real gap from a documentation gap.

**Exercise:** guided gap walkthrough. Downloadable 
worksheet. Revisits the Module 1 baseline.

---

## Module 5 - Talking to your board

**The reader's situation:** they have to present AI 
risk upward and have never done it.

Covers: what leadership actually asks, how to frame 
risk without alarming people or underselling it, how 
to present a gap position honestly, what to say when 
the answer is "we are not ready", how to ask for 
budget, and how to avoid committing to a 
certification timeline you cannot meet.

**Deliverable:** board briefing template.

**Assessed as the strongest and least served part 
of the offer.** Do not bury it.

---

## Module 6 - What next

**The reader's situation:** they now understand the 
position and need to decide what to do.

Covers: realistic options from do-nothing-yet 
through to full certification, what certification 
actually costs and how long it takes, when a 
documentation toolkit is the right buy and when it 
is not, when a consultant is worth it, and what the 
certification pathways look like for anyone who 
wants to go further.

**Honest note:** this module tells some readers not 
to spend more money. That is the right call and it 
is also what makes the rest credible.
